Deterministic HOSE-code-style 13C verifier (nmrshiftdb2-trained) — ablation
===========================================================================
Predictor: RDKit Morgan radial-environment bins (sphere r=4..1, min 3 samples,
hybridisation prior fallback), trained on nmrshiftdb2 assigned 13C spectra.
  training: 31,000 molecules, 332,595 assigned carbons, 263,366 environment bins
  held-out accuracy: MAE 3.23 ppm, median 1.73 ppm (17,456 carbons / 1,647 mols)

Re-ranking the §5.2 candidate set (60 compounds, 126 candidates):
  recall (true in candidate set):           19/60 (31%)
  top-1, solver self-rank:                  14/60 (23%)
  top-1, HOSE-verified re-rank:             14/60 (23%)
  conditional on recall (n=19):
    solver self-rank:                       14/19 (73%)
    HOSE-verified:                          14/19 (73%)
    LLM forward-verifier (for comparison):  16/19 (84%)

Why the deterministic verifier does NOT beat the LLM here — coverage:
  fraction of the 2,244 benchmark candidate carbons whose environment is matched
  at each sphere depth (deeper = more specific = more accurate):
    r=4: 2%   r=3: 27%   r=2: 39%   r=1: 28%   prior-fallback: 1%
  Only 2% match at the most specific sphere; 67% resolve only at coarse r<=2,
  which cannot distinguish the regioisomers the verifier must separate. The
  benchmark's exotic chemistry is under-represented in nmrshiftdb2, so a generic
  lookup predictor degrades to coarse environments exactly where discrimination
  is needed — whereas the LLM forward-predictor generalises across novel scaffolds.
Conclusion: the identified fix is NOT a generic HOSE lookup; it requires either
DFT-level (compound-specific) shift accuracy or orthogonal 2D-NMR constraints.
