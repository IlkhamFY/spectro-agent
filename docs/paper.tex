\hypertarget{introduction}{%
\section{1. Introduction}\label{introduction}}

Determining structure from spectra is central to synthetic and
analytical chemistry. Machine learning uses encoder--decoder models on
paired corpora; \emph{Spectro}, for one, learns ¹H/¹³C/IR → SELFIES from
6,833
molecules\textsuperscript{\protect\hyperlink{ref-chacko2024spectro}{1}}.
General-purpose LLMs are now reported off-the-shelf: a non-peer-reviewed
2026 industrial white paper found Claude Opus matching or beating
commercial NMR-prediction software forward (structure → spectrum, ±0.08
ppm ¹H) and ``recovering all eight simpler structures on every attempt''
in the
inverse\textsuperscript{\protect\hyperlink{ref-kamber2026chemist}{2}}.
We treat those numbers as a claim to test, not an established baseline.

That evaluation is narrow: 15 inverse problems on curated single-ring or
two-fragment molecules, NMR only, seven given the
\emph{starting-material structure} as a hint, and ``recovery'' scored
leniently over three runs and three ranked candidates. The chemist's
question --- \emph{take an arbitrary experimental spectrum from a paper
and recover the structure} --- remains open. It needs open experimental
data at scale, a blind benchmark others can report against, and honest
accounting of which stage of elucidation binds.

We provide all three. \emph{IRexp} (§2) is, to our knowledge, the
largest openly \emph{redistributable} collection of experimental IR
\emph{band lists} (121,233 records; 43,060 structure-linked; 33,201 IR +
¹H + ¹³C + structure); view-only libraries such as SDBS hold more
structure-linked \emph{spectra} but cannot be redistributed (§2.1).
\emph{IRSpectra-Bench} (§3) is the blind, mechanically scored benchmark
built from it --- 194 compounds, complexity-stratified, multimodal IR +
¹H + ¹³C. Concurrent 2026 suites bound related tasks (MolQuest scores
530 post-2025 compounds by exact canonical
SMILES\textsuperscript{\protect\hyperlink{ref-han2026molquest}{3}};
SpecX\textsuperscript{\protect\hyperlink{ref-xiang2026specx}{4}};
NMRGym\textsuperscript{\protect\hyperlink{ref-fang2026nmrgym}{5}}); ours
is an openly redistributable suite on literature-reported peak lists
with a recall/verification decomposition measured on Claude, replicated
across three other model families (§4.7), with a within-compound
solver-methodology control (§4). Ground truth is literature structures
resolved deterministically (OPSIN/RDKit) and checked mechanically
(560/560 bands on a seed-fixed sample, §2.3; expert-chemist review of
elucidation outputs is formally deferred, §8); no LLM curates labels or
scores predictions.

Forward-verification elucidation (§5) turns the model's \emph{strong}
direction (forward prediction) against its \emph{weak} one (inverse
regiochemistry); the loop is prior art (§1.1), the decomposition ours,
run training-free on every compound. The finding is sharp asymmetry:
given a candidate set containing it, the model \emph{verifies} the
correct structure 89\% of the time yet \emph{proposes} it for only 34\%,
so generation binds the result (Fig.~1). Gain is bounded (top-1 28\% →
30\% on n=194; 23\% → 30\% on the 60-compound arm where wide generation
was also tested) and, by our own measurements, cannot exceed a
recall/precision ceiling without sharper verification or 2D-NMR data.

We contribute IRexp and IRSpectra-Bench --- open peak lists plus
decomposable recall/verification metrics others can report --- measured
on every compound (34\% proposed; 89\% selected once proposed), with a
four-vendor replication of that split. The paper fills an accounting and
infrastructure gap: it diagnoses where elucidation binds; it does not
solve it.

\begin{figure*}
\centering
\includegraphics[width=6.3in,height=1.42in]{figures/fig_wall.pdf}
\caption{Diagnosis on IRSpectra-Bench (n=194): generation recall, not
verification, is the bottleneck. Where the true structure is never

which have different denominators and are not differenced.}

Solver and verifier (Fig.~S1) are LLM agents on a consumer subscription,
with no fine-tuning and no API spend. Inference is not exactly
reproducible --- no pinned snapshot, temperature or seed (§9) --- but
scoring is: predictions, ground truth and scorers are released, and
every training-free number regenerates from them. The HOSE lookup, GNN
(§5.4) and trained generator (§5.6) are fenced complements.

\hypertarget{related-work}{%
\subsection{1.1 Related work}\label{related-work}}

\textbf{Trained spectra → structure models.} The dominant line trains
sequence or graph decoders on paired corpora ---
\emph{Spectro}\textsuperscript{\protect\hyperlink{ref-chacko2024spectro}{1}},
multitask 1D-NMR
models\textsuperscript{\protect\hyperlink{ref-hu2024multitask}{6}},
NMRTrans/NMRSpec\textsuperscript{\protect\hyperlink{ref-yang2026nmrtrans}{7}},
\emph{NMIRacle} (IR + ¹H +
¹³C)\textsuperscript{\protect\hyperlink{ref-ottomano2025nmiracle}{8}}
--- accurate in-distribution but retrained per modality. IRexp (§2.3)
complements NMRSpec with redistributable IR band lists; neither corpus
alone is multimodal. Upstream IR work from our group
(\emph{vIR-OLO}\textsuperscript{\protect\hyperlink{ref-garcilazocruz2026virolo}{9}},
\emph{j-IR-vis}\textsuperscript{\protect\hyperlink{ref-sondhi2025jirvis}{10}})
identifies functional groups but does not generate candidate structures.

\textbf{LLMs as elucidators.} LLM agents already plan syntheses
(ChemCrow\textsuperscript{\protect\hyperlink{ref-mbran2024chemcrow}{11}},
Coscientist\textsuperscript{\protect\hyperlink{ref-boiko2023coscientist}{12}})
and call elucidation routines; we measure what one returns.
Off-the-shelf and multimodal
LLMs\textsuperscript{\protect\hyperlink{ref-kamber2026chemist}{2},\protect\hyperlink{ref-su2025spectrallm}{13}--\protect\hyperlink{ref-zhuang2025treesearch}{15}},
puzzle benchmarks
(MolPuzzle\textsuperscript{\protect\hyperlink{ref-guo2024molpuzzle}{16}}),
and agentic IR interpreters
(IR-Agent\textsuperscript{\protect\hyperlink{ref-noh2025iragent}{17}};
Priessner \emph{et
al.}\textsuperscript{\protect\hyperlink{ref-priessner2026reasoning}{18}})
improve \emph{how} a model reads spectra or re-ranks a fixed pool; we
ask which stage binds once it has read them.

Most benchmarks use simulated or single-instrument
spectra\textsuperscript{\protect\hyperlink{ref-chacko2024spectro}{1},\protect\hyperlink{ref-ottomano2025nmiracle}{8},\protect\hyperlink{ref-alberts2024ir}{19},\protect\hyperlink{ref-alberts2025benchmarks}{20}};
IRSpectra-Bench uses literature-reported peak lists across thousands of
laboratories. Cross-paper numbers swing with harness: GPT-4o scores
1.4\% on
MolPuzzle\textsuperscript{\protect\hyperlink{ref-guo2024molpuzzle}{16}},
27.8\% with chain-of-thought, 57.8\% with
tree-search\textsuperscript{\protect\hyperlink{ref-zhuang2025treesearch}{15}}.
We fix one pre-registered RDKit-InChIKey protocol with bootstrap CIs.

\textbf{Forward verification and CASE.} Matching predicted to observed
shifts ---
DP4\textsuperscript{\protect\hyperlink{ref-smith2010dp4}{21},\protect\hyperlink{ref-grimblat2015dp4plus}{22}},
NMR
crystallography\textsuperscript{\protect\hyperlink{ref-pickard2001gipaw}{23},\protect\hyperlink{ref-ashbrook2016nmrcryst}{24}},
CASE\textsuperscript{\protect\hyperlink{ref-elyashberg2012case}{25},\protect\hyperlink{ref-elyashberg2015case}{26}}
--- is easier than inverse generation. We swap DFT for a forward LLM.
CASE enumerators have exhaustive recall by construction; the LLM
literature almost never reports whether the true structure was proposed
at all (§6).

\textbf{Prior loops and concurrent work.}
\emph{NMR-Solver}\textsuperscript{\protect\hyperlink{ref-jin2025nmrsolver}{27}}
runs the same generate-and-verify loop without an LLM (52.9\% top-1 on
literature ¹H/¹³C, formula supplied; 31.6\% with stereochemistry). We
decompose error rather than beat that score.
\emph{NMRAgent}\textsuperscript{\protect\hyperlink{ref-fang2026nmragent}{28}}
is a complementary best-effort system. Kliuev \emph{et al.} place the
best of six LLMs at 24\% on 105 peak
lists\textsuperscript{\protect\hyperlink{ref-kliuev2026canai}{29}}.
Espejo Morales \emph{et al.} frame agentic search with processing tools
and hard checks: 80.9\% on education spectra, 20.6\% on 34 industrial
samples\textsuperscript{\protect\hyperlink{ref-espejo2026agentic}{30}}
--- they ask which architecture; we ask which stage binds. Their inputs
are raw instrument files; ours are published reports (§8). Priessner
\emph{et al.} already state that re-ranking cannot recover a missing
candidate\textsuperscript{\protect\hyperlink{ref-priessner2026reasoning}{18}}.
We add measurement at scale: the split on 194 compounds across four
model families and four verifiers, recovered from published top-\emph{k}
figures (§6).

\hypertarget{the-irexp-dataset}{%
\section{2. The IRexp dataset}\label{the-irexp-dataset}}

\hypertarget{motivation}{%
\subsection{2.1 Motivation}\label{motivation}}

An IRexp record is a \emph{band list} --- peak positions in cm⁻¹
transcribed by an author into a paper's text --- with co-reported ¹H/¹³C
shift lists and, where resolvable, the 2D structure. IRexp contains no
absorbance traces. That is the published form a language model consumes,
but a different object from a digitised spectrum, and the two should not
be counted against one another.

Among \emph{digitised spectra}, free downloads run to the NIST
WebBook\textsuperscript{\protect\hyperlink{ref-nist_webbook}{31}} (≈16k)
and the Chemotion ELN
deposit\textsuperscript{\protect\hyperlink{ref-chemotion2024}{32}}
(≈2k). AIST SDBS\textsuperscript{\protect\hyperlink{ref-sdbs}{33}} is
larger (≈54k FT-IR, all structure-linked) but \emph{view-only} (50
spectra/day, no bulk export), so IRexp does not compete there: SDBS
alone holds more structure-linked spectra than IRexp holds
structure-linked band lists. Among text-derived \emph{band lists} IRexp
is the largest \emph{openly redistributable} collection by record count
--- deliberately scoped to that object type.

\hypertarget{construction}{%
\subsection{2.2 Construction}\label{construction}}

Experimental sections report per-compound IR band lists with co-reported
¹H/¹³C NMR in a stable textual convention. Mining it is
mature\textsuperscript{\protect\hyperlink{ref-swain2016chemdataextractor}{34}};
we add a pipeline specialised to the \emph{IR band list}, the modality
those tools passed over.

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Discovery.} Open-access literature is harvested in bulk from
  the PMC Open-Access
  Subset\textsuperscript{\protect\hyperlink{ref-pmc_oa}{35}} and the
  Chemotion FT-IR deposit (RADAR4Chem).
\item
  \textbf{Extraction.} A deterministic parser segments experimental text
  per compound and extracts IR wavenumbers and ¹H/¹³C shifts, gated
  against scan-range artefacts and prose false-positives.
\item
  \textbf{Resolution.} IUPAC names go to SMILES with
  OPSIN\textsuperscript{\protect\hyperlink{ref-lowe2011opsin}{36}} and
  RDKit\textsuperscript{\protect\hyperlink{ref-landrum_rdkit}{37}}
  canonicalisation (InChIKey,
  SELFIES\textsuperscript{\protect\hyperlink{ref-krenn2020selfies}{38}}),
  with a
  PubChem\textsuperscript{\protect\hyperlink{ref-kim2023pubchem}{39}}
  fallback for trivial names; handling open-access label conventions and
  narrative artefacts raised structure coverage from 24\% to 35\%.
\end{itemize}

The pools differ: PMC records are author-transcribed (median 9 bands),
Chemotion records peak-picked from deposited spectra (median 39); users
training on band density should treat them apart.
\texttt{scripts/\hspace{0pt}split\_license\_pools.py} separates them on
\texttt{source\_doi} and stamps each record's licence.

\textbf{Extraction fidelity is measured.} On a seed-fixed random sample
of 60 PMC-sourced records
(\texttt{scripts/\hspace{0pt}audit\_extraction.py\ -\/-n\ 60\ -\/-seed\ 0})
we re-fetched each article and checked every recorded wavenumber against
its text: 560/560 bands and 60/60 records were confirmed (Wilson 95\% CI
99.3--100\% and 94--100\%). This bounds \emph{transcription} error ---
hallucinated, mis-parsed or unit-mangled values --- below 1\% of bands.
It does not measure whether the parser found every IR string in every
paper --- a recall question requiring human reading; that
extraction-recall audit is formally deferred (§8).

\texttt{irexp\_resolved} (43,060 records, 100\% structure-linked) is the
benchmark-ready split, ≈6× the 6,833-molecule set used to train
Spectro\textsuperscript{\protect\hyperlink{ref-chacko2024spectro}{1}}
(Fig.~S2).

\hypertarget{benchmark-design-irspectra-bench}{%
\section{3. Benchmark design
(IRSpectra-Bench)}\label{benchmark-design-irspectra-bench}}

From \texttt{irexp\_resolved} we draw \emph{IRSpectra-Bench}, 194 blind
elucidation problems, each giving the \emph{molecular formula} (as from
HRMS), the \emph{IR band list} and the \emph{¹H and ¹³C shift lists}; no
name, SMILES or scaffold hint. In 10 of the 194 the authors' peak
assignments name a ring system (\texttt{2CH2·pyrrolidine},
\texttt{pyridazinone\ H5}). Those compounds are solved 1/10 against
54/184 (29.3\%) for the rest
(\texttt{scripts/\hspace{0pt}prompt\_leakage.py}), so the annotation
does not carry the headline; we keep them rather than drop them.
Main-round ground truths are spectrally validated by an automated RDKit
check (¹³C peaks vs symmetry-unique carbons, formula match, SELFIES
round-trip) excluding merged or incomplete spectra --- 6/140, leaving
134; \texttt{scripts/\hspace{0pt}validate\_benchmark.py} applies the
same filter to all three rounds, regenerating every
\texttt{clean\_qids.json} from the released questions and answers.

The filter does not gate on ¹H. In 13 of the 194 retained records the
reported ¹H integral exceeds the reference structure's hydrogen count
--- residual solvent, water, exchangeable protons, or a rotamer mixture
--- printed as a diagnostic, not excluded; the cohort was fixed in
advance, and re-filtering post hoc on a criterion chosen after seeing
results is a degree of freedom a benchmark should not take. We report
the sensitivity instead: dropping all 13 moves the headline from 28.4\%
to 29.3\% (53/181), +0.9 points, leaving every conclusion unchanged. The
controlled rounds' 60 compounds are fixed, pre-registered sets, audited
the same way but reported rather than filtered (57/60 pass; §8).

\textbf{Difficulty is declared in advance, as a property of the
compound.} By RDKit ring analysis a compound is \emph{simple} iff it has
at most two rings, no fused/spiro/bridgehead system and ≤22 heavy atoms;
all others are \emph{complex} (98 simple / 96 complex). The threshold is
not load-bearing: sweeping it from 18 to 26 leaves the
simple-minus-complex top-1 gap inside a 36--40-point band throughout
(36.1 at the narrowest, 39.6 as released;
\texttt{scripts/\hspace{0pt}difficulty\_sensitivity.py}). This axis is
distinct from the continuous size gradient of §4.1 (≤15 / 16--25 /
\textgreater25 heavy atoms), and InChIKey de-duplication across rounds
prevents leakage.

\textbf{The 50/50 balance is by design.} The sampler fills each
difficulty stratum to half the round; the eligible corpus is 17.5\%
simple / 82.5\% complex. Reweighted to that composition, top-1 falls
28.4\% → 15.2\% {[}10.6--20.4{]} and recall 33.5\% → 19.8\%
{[}14.4--25.8{]}; verification precision must be reweighted too (89.2\%
→ 71.5\% {[}50.2--92.5{]}). We report the benchmark figure as released
and the reweighted figure as the better estimate on an arbitrary paper.

\textbf{Scoring is mechanical.} A prediction is \emph{correct} if its
RDKit InChIKey connectivity layer (first 14 characters) matches the
reference: we score \emph{constitution}, so correct constitution with
wrong stereochemistry counts as correct. The \emph{full},
stereochemistry-sensitive InChIKey gives 21.1\% top-1 and 25.8\%
recovered (41/194 and 50/194) against 28.4\% / 33.5\% --- 7.3 points
lower on top-1 and 7.7 on recovered --- so the constitution figure is an
upper bound on full-stereochemistry accuracy. Constitution is the
headline because 1D ¹H/¹³C/IR rarely fixes absolute configuration: only
10.3\% (20/194) of answers carry a \emph{defined} (assigned R/S)
stereocentre, and a model is penalised there for information the prompt
never contained (\texttt{scripts/score\_main.py\ -\/-stereo}).

\textbf{Metrics.} \emph{Top-1 (exact constitution)}: best-ranked
candidate matches at the connectivity layer; \emph{recovered (top-3)}:
reference among up to three returned candidates, the lenient
``recovery'' protocol of
ref.\textsuperscript{\protect\hyperlink{ref-kamber2026chemist}{2}}.
\emph{Generation recall}: reference in the candidate pool \emph{before}
re-ranking, the ceiling any verifier can reach (coincides with recovered
except in §5.3, where the pool is larger). \emph{Verification precision
(conditional on recall)} over recall-positive compounds isolates
verification from generation. The forward-verifier ranks by symmetric
\emph{chamfer distance} between predicted and observed ¹³C peak sets
(lower is better); Morgan(2, 2048)
Tanimoto\textsuperscript{\protect\hyperlink{ref-rogers2010ecfp}{40}}
gives a graded scaffold-family signal. CIs are bootstrap 95\%;
model-vs-model differences use McNemar's exact
test\textsuperscript{\protect\hyperlink{ref-mcnemar1947}{41}} with Holm
correction\textsuperscript{\protect\hyperlink{ref-holm1979}{42}}.

Solvers are frontier LLM agents (Claude Opus), one sub-agent per batch,
closed-book under a consumer subscription: no tools beyond an RDKit
formula check, no ground truth, verified by grep-auditing transcripts.
Formula-adherence rates by round are reported in the Electronic
Supplementary Information.

\hypertarget{forward-verification-elucidation}{%
\section{5. Forward-verification
elucidation}\label{forward-verification-elucidation}}

\hypertarget{method}{%
\subsection{5.1 Method}\label{method}}

The inverse direction is the model's hard, isomer-blind direction; the
forward direction (structure → spectrum) is its easy, accurate
one\textsuperscript{\protect\hyperlink{ref-kamber2026chemist}{2}}. We
close a training-free generator--verifier loop:

\begin{quote}
\emph{generate} candidate structures (inverse) → \emph{forward-predict}
each candidate's ¹³C spectrum, blind to the observed spectrum →
\emph{re-rank} candidates by the distance between predicted and observed
¹³C → return the best match.
\end{quote}

The inverse solver's 373 deduplicated candidates across 194 targets are
forward-predicted in shuffled, anonymised batches, blind to the observed
spectrum and target identity. Predicted and observed ¹³C peak sets are
compared by symmetric chamfer distance (Fig.~5).

\begin{figure*}
\centering
\includegraphics[width=6.3in,height=3.85in]{figures/fig_mechanism.pdf}
\caption{Forward-verification on a benchmark regioisomer pair:
picolinamide and nicotinamide are indistinguishable to the inverse task,
but forward-predicted ¹³C sticks separate the true isomer from the
alternative (§5.1).}
\end{figure*}

Regioisomers separate by a median 1.21 ppm in forward prediction, but
82\% lie within the predictor's ≈2 ppm error --- a thin margin confirmed
against a derangement null at p=0.001 (§5.5). Sharper predictors (GNN,
DFT-coupled
models{[}\textsuperscript{\protect\hyperlink{ref-williamson2024mpnn}{44}};\textsuperscript{\protect\hyperlink{ref-han2024dftgnn}{45},\protect\hyperlink{ref-cohen2023delta50}{46}})
would move the verification ceiling, not search.

reported null.

\hypertarget{is-the-recall-wall-task-intrinsic-a-trained-generator-probe}{%
\subsection{5.6 Is the recall wall task-intrinsic? A trained-generator
probe}\label{is-the-recall-wall-task-intrinsic-a-trained-generator-probe}}

That ceiling is a property of training-free LLM elicitation, not the
task. Pooling a small IRexp-fine-tuned ¹H/¹³C → SMILES generator with
Claude's candidates lifts recall 33.5\% → 54.1\% and top-1 28.4\% →
35.1\% (McNemar p=0.015; Fig.~S5). Zero-shot without IRexp fine-tuning
recovers 0/248; with it, 25\%. NMR-Solver
(52.9\%)\textsuperscript{\protect\hyperlink{ref-jin2025nmrsolver}{27}}
and trained IR transformers
(63.8\%)\textsuperscript{\protect\hyperlink{ref-alberts2025benchmarks}{20}}
confirm the task is not bound at 28--30\%.

\hypertarget{discussion}{%
\section{7. Discussion}\label{discussion}}

Frontier LLMs are good verifiers (89\% conditional precision) and weak
proposers (34\% recall) on real literature spectra. The contribution is
a diagnosis with a bounded, training-free improvement, not a solved
elucidator (§8).

That split held across four Claude models, a battery-electrolyte subset,
four verifiers and four vendor families (§6); concurrent systems that
move the same levers are surveyed in §1.1.

Training is not foreclosed: IRexp fine-tuning lifts recall to 54\%
(§5.6). What compounds is open experimental data, honest benchmarks, and
inference scaffolding that rides each new model. Two practical findings:
bounded contexts with tool access may help (§4.3); use forward-predicted
¹³C agreement to re-rank, not as an abstention gauge (§5.5).

\hypertarget{limitations}{%
\section{8. Limitations}\label{limitations}}

Scoring is mechanical; solver runs were transcript-audited for zero
ground-truth access (transcripts on request). \textbf{(i) Consumer
harness.} Runs used a consumer subscription that exposes no model
snapshot, temperature, seed or thinking tier; exact inference is not
reproducible. Scoring is: frozen predictions, ground truth and
mechanical scorers regenerate every training-free number. The
instruction text wrapping Claude solver and forward-prediction batches
was not captured --- only per-compound payloads are released --- so the
verbatim prompts in the ESI are the cross-vendor harness prompts.
\textbf{(ii) Pretraining contamination} is bounded by formula-only (23\%
→ 5\%) and flat recency controls (§4.6) but not excluded; verbatim
spectral strings from PMC in the prompt remain a retrieval confound.
\textbf{(iii) Object type, formula and scoring.} Inputs are
author-transcribed band and shift lists, not raw absorbance traces or
FIDs --- a different object from digitised spectra and from Espejo
\emph{et
al.}\textsuperscript{\protect\hyperlink{ref-espejo2026agentic}{30}}. The
molecular formula is supplied (as from HRMS); systems such as
NMIRacle\textsuperscript{\protect\hyperlink{ref-ottomano2025nmiracle}{8}}
that take no formula solve a harder prior, so absolute accuracies are
not interchangeable. Headline metrics score constitution (InChIKey
connectivity); with stereochemistry, top-1 is 21.1\%. \textbf{(iv)
Statistical honesty.} Forward-verification vs self-ranking is unresolved
(McNemar p=0.55); generate-wide top-1 likewise (p=0.34). The four-model
comparison at n=24 is underpowered for adjacent ranks and carries a
disclosed protocol asymmetry (§4.4). The inference ladder diagnoses a
bottleneck; it is not a demonstrated accuracy advance. \textbf{(v)
Missing on-bench baselines.} Spectro, NMIRacle and CASE were not scored
on IRSpectra-Bench, so the LLM recall wall is not cleanly separated from
harness or modality choice --- left to future leaderboard work.
\textbf{(vi) Cross-vendor scope.} Headline n=194 is Claude Opus; the
four-vendor replication is on 60 compounds. Candidate budgets differ
across vendors (Claude mean 2.20 vs 3.00), so recall rankings are
approximate (§4.7). \textbf{(vii) Deferred and abandoned arms.} The
expert-chemist audit of elucidation outputs is frozen at
\texttt{data/\hspace{0pt}audit/} and formally deferred --- not run.
Leave-one-modality-out ablation
(\texttt{noIR}/\texttt{noH}/\texttt{noC}) was specified and never
completed; it is abandoned for this manuscript
(\texttt{docs/\hspace{0pt}MODELS.md}; ESI). The extraction-recall human
audit of parser coverage (§2.3) is likewise deferred. \textbf{(viii)
Scope.} Battery subset uses literature electrolyte chemistry, not
operando spectra. Single-sample scoring per compound (bootstrap CIs
reflect compound sampling only). Organic literature bias of PMC-OA
sources.

\hypertarget{methods}{%
\section{9. Methods}\label{methods}}

\textbf{Mining and resolution.} PMC-OA full text was fetched from
\texttt{s3:/\hspace{0pt}/\hspace{0pt}pmc-oa-opendata}, parsed
deterministically, and resolved with OPSIN, RDKit and SELFIES, with an
optional cached PubChem fallback.

\textbf{Benchmark and agents.} Problems were sampled from
\texttt{irexp\_resolved}, stratified by RDKit ring analysis, and
de-duplicated across rounds by InChIKey. A compound was admitted only if
its raw ¹H payload --- multiplicities and \emph{J} values as printed ---
could be re-extracted verbatim from the PMC-OA full text of its source
article (\texttt{benchmark\_v2.raw\_1h\_for}), which puts genuine
coupling information in the prompt and, unavoidably, the source
article's exact string (§8). The battery-electrolyte subset (§4.5) was
drawn by SMARTS filters for six electrolyte functional classes
(carbonate, sulfonyl/sulfonate, nitrile, sp³-C--F, phosphoryl,
glyme/oligoether), balanced to eight compounds per class (48 curated; 46
scored after two yielded no parseable candidate), excluding compounds
used elsewhere; it was \emph{J}-enriched and spectrally validated.
Solver and forward-prediction agents were independent Claude-Opus
sub-agents under the same subscription, instructed closed-book and
audited for zero web/answer access. Scoring used RDKit
InChIKey-connectivity match; similarity Morgan(2, 2048) Tanimoto;
forward-verification a symmetric chamfer distance over ¹³C peak sets.
The core protocol trains no model and uses no paid API; the §5.6
generator and the §5.4 learned ¹³C verifier are the only trained
components, reported as complements and fenced from the headline
results.

\textbf{Models and versions.} Headline runs used Claude Opus via
consumer subscription (2026-06-09--11); formula-only control 2026-07-28;
forward-prediction extension 2026-08-07. No model snapshot or decoding
parameters can be reported --- the harness exposes neither (§9). Fixed
are frozen per-compound outputs and mechanical scorers
(\texttt{docs/\hspace{0pt}MODELS.md}).

\textbf{Reproducibility.} Every round is frozen: questions, ground-truth
answers, per-agent raw outputs, predictions and scorer outputs are
released; the sampler, scorer and forward-verification harness are
scripted end-to-end.
